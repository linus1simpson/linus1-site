The Dispatch

April 15, 2026  ·  Life

"How do you do it?
How do you get paid to do what you want?"

No one's ever asked me that. But I wish they would.

Right now, I sit at home, scroll too much, and wish things were different. I want more, but I don't always act on it. My focus slips. I start things — music, ideas, plans — but I don't always follow through.

This is me trying to change that.

I have a degree in recording arts. I speak Spanish. I've worked with people, with kids. But I haven't tapped into real, focused work. It's all felt surface level. What I actually want is purpose — something that makes my days feel necessary.

Life isn't going to hand me the perfect situation that combines everything I love — music, nature, teaching, community. I have to build that myself. And maybe what's meant for me will come — but only if I'm moving enough to catch it.

That means doing the right things: exercising, learning, talking to people, getting uncomfortable. Actually doing.

Because right now, I see the problem clearly: I consume, I wish, and I hesitate. And that creates a loop of negativity. I don't want that.

Everything can be turned into something positive — but only if you choose to see it that way. Everything is a chance to learn. You can always turn things around. But it starts with action.

So today:
I'll write.
I'll scroll less.
I'll make something.

If I can do that consistently, then every day becomes something to build on.

That's the point: your current situation doesn't define you. The ability to try does.

Your mind can expand as far as you let it. But if you don't believe that — or don't act on it — you'll stay exactly where you are.

Nothing about this idea is new. People have been saying it forever. The difference now is simple: the ones who try, who experiment, who improve, who stay open — they're the ones who move forward.

So the real question is

Will you start?

May 2026  ·  Basketball · Mental Health

Keep Fighting. Get After It.

"Growth doesn't ask permission. It just asks if you're showing up."

There's something I keep coming back to — the idea that your mental state is directly connected to how much you're moving, creating, and pushing yourself forward. Not in a hustle-culture way. In a real, human way. The days I feel best are the ones where I went outside, did something physical, worked on something that matters. The days I feel worst are the ones where I didn't.

Switch things up. Go outside. Notice what's beautiful about the world. These aren't clichés — they're actual tools. Self-esteem isn't built by thinking about yourself differently. It's built by doing things and seeing yourself do them.

Basketball taught me that. You don't get better at the game by wanting to be better at the game. You get better by going to the gym, again and again, when nobody's watching and nobody's clapping. That consistency — that quiet, unglamorous consistency — is the thing that actually builds you.

On the Court — Playoffs 2026

Oklahoma City is going to win this thing. I don't want them to. But depth wins championships, and right now OKC has the most complete roster in the league — Jared McCain bringing youth and energy, Caruso bringing the veteran grit, and Shai at the top holding it all together. He won the MVP. But let's be honest about how he gets there. Shai has turned flopping into an art form. He studies the defender's momentum, gets them leaning backward, then stops dead and lets them run into him. He flails on contact in a way that is practiced and calculated. The refs have to call it — that's the reality of the modern game. And it's spread. His teammates do it. The whole league has adapted. Now I see kids at every level doing the same thing — complaining, drawing contact, talking instead of playing.

I'm not sure it's the most positive development in basketball. But I also can't ignore what the game has become overall. More points per game than ever. Finesse at a level players from thirty years ago wouldn't have imagined possible. You don't just need force anymore — you need mental conditioning, footwork, and the discipline to stay in front of these athletes play after play. In that way the game has genuinely progressed. Steph made kids shoot threes from half court in warmups. Every era reshapes what the next generation thinks is possible.

Victor Wembanyama is the best player on earth. I believe that. The MVP went to Shai but Wemby is on a different level — a player that shouldn't exist, doing things the game has never seen at that size. The Spurs are young, they are building, and this will not be the last time we see them in a conference finals. Wemby has something to prove and that motivation alone makes him one of the most exciting players to watch in a long time.

I'm a Laker fan. I have no business rooting for San Antonio. But here we are.

The thread between all of this — the mental health piece, the basketball piece, building this site — is the same thing. Keep fighting. Keep showing up. Don't wait until you feel ready because that feeling doesn't come first. The readiness comes from doing it anyway.

Get after it.

July 2026  ·  Food · Hometown

The San Juan Taco Crawl

I love my city. San Juan Capistrano is small, but the food coming out of this town is serious — barbecue, pizza, Japanese, we've got it all. Today, though, I want to talk about Mexican food.

My family and I decided to rate the tacos from every Mexican restaurant in San Juan. One taco per spot, on foot, working our way across town. A proper crawl. We were not disappointed — honestly, you can't really go wrong at any of them. But a few stood out enough that these are the ones I'd walk you to if you came to visit.

El Coyotito — 9/10

One of my personal favorites. They put homemade, traditional flavor right up front: handmade tortillas, fresh everything, meat that turns a simple taco into something you remember on the drive home. It isn't complicated, and it doesn't need to be. The seasoning is just right. Simple and delicious is harder to pull off than it sounds.

El Molino — the burrito spot

Their tacos might not top the list, but this is my favorite place in town for a burrito, full stop. In the morning, it's the breakfast burrito with chorizo y papas — my favorite thing on this earth to eat, and I've been ordering it my whole life. They tuck beans inside, which I don't think is all that common, but now I expect beans in any breakfast burrito I eat. Chorizo scrambled into the eggs, potatoes stacked on top, cilantro and onion, sour cream, cheese, and that green salsa — the green salsa I could genuinely drink. The whole thing just flows. Come lunch or dinner, it's a classic al pastor burrito and an agua fresca: banana, watermelon, or melon. Satisfying every single time.

El Zarandeado Mexican Seafood — 10/10

The best taco in San Juan, and it isn't close. The fish taco is unreal — fresh, clean, exactly what it should be, no notes. The shrimp is excellent and the margaritas don't miss either. Here's the thing, though: it's underrated. There's no giant sign out front, so people walk right past it. But the folks inside are kind, the cooks take real pride in their work, and you can taste it in every bite. Go.

Honorable mentions: Las Costas, Ricardo's Place, Cancun, and El Campeón — all worth your time.

But those first three? Those are where I'm taking you.

San Juan grows a little every year, and I really do believe we're becoming the culinary capital of Orange County. Come eat. See for yourself.

— L

July 2026  ·  Travel

Small in the Best Way

Everyone needs to see Yosemite once. I mean that.

It's a valley walled in by colossal faces of rock — Half Dome, El Capitan, the ones everybody knows. But the rock isn't the whole story. It's everything standing together: green trees, wild plants, the whole living valley floor dwarfed by stone that has been there longer than any of us can wrap our heads around. It makes you feel small. And I mean that as the best thing that can happen to a person.

You stand down there and it lands — I'm not as big as I think I am. The world doesn't revolve around me. And somehow that's a relief. The weight slides off your shoulders, and all that's left to do is look up and take it in.

Then there are the climbers. It's insane that people go up those walls — even with ropes, insane. Hours of pure focus, locked in, never looking down, never making a single mistake. And the grip strength — hanging off granite by your fingertips. I don't understand people like Alex Honnold, who free solo it with no rope at all. I just applaud them. It's what they love, and there's something beautiful in loving a thing that much. I kept wishing I'd brought binoculars to catch them mid-climb — apparently some of them even sleep up there, strapped to the face for the night. Nuts.

I went for two nights with good friends. I drove Arman and Derek up, and we celebrated Dillon and Arman's birthdays out there. We stayed in a tent cabin in Curry Village — community showers, clean, comfortable. More glamping than camping, honestly, and I have zero complaints. We ate pizza in the village both nights: a joy the first time, a little much by the second.

We earned our miles. The day we swam at Cathedral Beach was its own kind of perfect — cold, clear water, exactly what your body wants after a day in the sun. The next day, Cathedral Lakes Trail: about eight and a half miles winding through the trees until it opened onto a lake that felt like an oasis. Water, mountains, trees, all of it — and not another soul in sight. That's the thing about me and vacations. I'd rather get off the grid than deeper into it. Walk, look, talk, think. Nothing calms me the same way.

Our last day took us out toward Hetch Hetchy, a hike around the reservoir to a waterfall. Beautiful in its own right — that vast body of water pressed against the rock — though it carries a different air back there. Hotter, drier, like the moisture got wrung out of it. I have my questions about whether that dam is the best thing for the land, but I'll leave those for another day. We met up with my buddy John and his girlfriend out there and had a great time in it regardless.

We closed the trip with two days in San Francisco during Pride — wandering the city, catching up with friends who live up there. A good way to come down off the mountain.

We met a guy named Dale in Yosemite. He's taking his mom to every national park in the country, and beyond. The man is glowing about it. And I got it. That's what I carried home: getting outside and putting my hands on the natural world genuinely grounds me. It makes me feel whole. I need to keep making the effort — especially with friends, because they're what make it better.

Fourteen hours of driving, round trip. Worth every mile, and I'll do it again. Just picked up a pair of Merrells, so I'm ready.

More to come.

— L

July 2026  ·  Basketball

The Offseason Never Sleeps

The season's over, but the game doesn't stop. The draft, the trades, the chaos — this is where next year gets built, and there's a lot to unpack.

Let's talk Lakers. LeBron is gone. He told the team he won't be back, and man — I'll always glaze King Bron. I loved every minute of that era. Part of me wishes there were a world where he stayed on a vet minimum and rode into the Luka era as the final piece. Storybook stuff. But his number was never going to fit what we're building, and as much as it stings, we needed a clean slate. Laker fans don't do content. It's a championship or it's nothing.

Here's why I'm actually excited, though: Luka with a real big man is already a recipe for damage. That's his whole game. He tortures teams in the pick-and-roll — sucks the defense in, picks it apart, and springs everybody else open in the process. Give him a true rim-runner and it's a wrap. That's exactly what Walker Kessler is: a lob threat, a rim protector, a guy who dives hard and finishes everything. If those two build real chemistry, that connection could be lethal. Add Mamu, Sexton, and Grimes, and there's a genuine roster here.

Re-signing Reaves to the max ($185M) wasn't my dream move — I like Austin, but he has to prove it when the lights are brightest, in the playoffs, and lately he hasn't. Still, get me one more swing — a Kuminga, a Peyton Watson — and we're dangerous. And don't tell me LeBron isn't quietly plotting a vet-minimum ring chase out West. I'd welcome him home in a heartbeat.

The one that stings: losing Rui. That man looked like the best shooter on the planet at stretches in the playoffs, and he's just a genuinely good dude. I wanted to wear his jersey in Japan this September with the boys. I still might.

But let's be honest about the mountain. The Thunder and the Spurs are leading this race, and probably for years. Those rosters are deep, dynamic, and tough defensively — the exact thing the Lakers have lacked lately. Everybody in the West is trying to solve Wemby and OKC right now, and it isn't close. That's the standard. That's what we're chasing.

The rest of the league is a movie too — Giannis to Miami, Ja to Portland, LaMelo to Minnesota, Jaylen Brown to Philly. Everybody's loading up. It's the same energy I'm carrying into the studio: a new song in the works, the World Cup on home soil, everything in motion at once. And that's exactly how I like it.

Keep moving. — L

May 2026  ·  Origin

Where This Came From

August 2022. Palomar Mountain. The Cleveland National Forest. A camping trip with eight of my closest friends, a hiking trail up toward the observatory, and a mushroom tea that hit a little harder than expected.

Somewhere on that trail, overwhelmed and rambling, I started talking about a website. A place where all of us could bring what we're good at and put it in one room. Sales and fashion and art and music and writing and code and conversation — all of it, together, under one roof. A lounge.

I meant every word of it.

The People On That Trail

John — spots things before anyone else does. Trends, deals, value that other people walk past. His taste is his own and he backs it.

Austin — the reason this site exists in the form it does. He built his first. Poems, stories, ideas out in the open for people to read and respond to while he earns his masters in creative writing and chases a life in teaching. He makes things happen because he decides to.

Boone — analytical and creative at the same time, which is rare. Codes his way through problems. Always thinking about the experience, his own and everyone else's.

Derek — knows things and knows people. The kind of wit and work ethic that makes success feel inevitable for him. Easy to be around, hard to forget.

Ethan — unpredictable in the best way. Can talk to anyone. Shows up every day and does the work. That kind of energy is contagious and harder to find than people think.

Jacob — his eye for aesthetics is underrated. Adapts to any room, any situation. Has real opinions about visuals and isn't afraid to share them. Every brand needs someone like that.

Peyton — quiet about it but capable of anything he decides to pursue. Tough and creative and the kind of person who lends a hand without needing to make it a thing.

Arman — entrepreneurial to his core. Talks to anyone, reads the room, stays ahead of what's coming. Always moving, always growing, always pulling people along with him. He was part of why I started when I did.

I'm building this alone right now and that's fine. That's how most things start. But the original idea was always collective — a place where people with different abilities could combine them into something real.

That idea didn't go away when we came down from the mountain. It just needed time. New songs will go here. New experiences. New people. It will grow with me.

This is where it started. A trail, eight friends, and one rambling idea that turned out to be worth something.
